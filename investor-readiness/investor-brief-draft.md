# B9TECH CONSULTS — Investor Brief
## Working thesis and pitch-deck structure

**Version:** 0.1  
**Prepared:** 3 November 2026  
**Status:** Internal strategy draft; not an offer to sell securities.

## 1. Executive thesis

B9TECH is being positioned around two complementary engines:

- **Enterprise services:** technology advisory, AI and data science implementation, software engineering, cybersecurity-aware delivery and workflow automation.
- **Proprietary products:** product development initiatives, currently represented publicly by B3: BringBackBarter.

The strategic hypothesis is that services can generate early commercial relationships, cash flow and practical understanding of customer problems, while carefully selected proprietary products may create repeatable and scalable intellectual property.

This is a hypothesis to validate—not a proven business model or investment return.

## 2. Problem space

Organisations may struggle to translate technology interest into business outcomes because of fragmented data, unclear use cases, integration complexity, limited internal capacity, security concerns and weak change management.

B9TECH must validate which customer segments experience these problems most acutely, which problems carry budget, and which can be solved profitably.

## 3. Solution and capability

The current public service portfolio covers:
- AI strategy and implementation
- Data science and decision intelligence
- Software and digital product engineering
- Cybersecurity and responsible technology
- Automation and digital transformation
- Technology advisory and delivery support

The offer should be narrowed to two or three initial buyer segments and packaged into specific engagements with scope, duration, deliverables, price logic and measurable outcomes.

## 4. Product strategy: B3

B3: BringBackBarter is the public product initiative. The investor case requires a current product demonstration and evidence covering:
- User problem and target market
- Actual product functionality versus roadmap
- User acquisition and activation
- Listings, matches and completed exchanges
- Repeat usage and retention
- Trust, dispute and safety processes
- Unit economics and operational costs
- IP ownership and technical dependencies

No traction figures should be published until verified from source systems and defined consistently.

## 5. Business model hypotheses

Potential service revenue:
- Fixed-scope discovery and readiness assessments
- Project-based implementation
- Retainers for advisory and ongoing technical support
- Managed support, only where service capacity and SLAs exist

Potential product revenue for B3 (to validate):
- Optional premium features
- Business or institutional accounts
- Transaction-adjacent service fees, only if the product design and user economics support them
- Partnerships or sponsored programmes, where commercially and legally appropriate

Do not assume any of these product revenue streams are live. Test willingness to pay and regulatory implications before forecasting them.

## 6. Go-to-market hypothesis

**Enterprise:** founder-led outreach, targeted discovery conversations, partner referrals, paid diagnostics and conversion into scoped delivery projects.

**B3:** select a narrow initial user segment and geography; validate liquidity, repeat exchange behaviour, trust and customer acquisition economics before scaling.

## 7. Competitive positioning

The company should compete on problem understanding, execution discipline, responsible technology, local context and transparent delivery—not broad claims of being the largest, first or most advanced.

A credible competitor map requires named alternatives, buyer interviews, pricing evidence, switching costs and differentiated proof.

## 8. Financial story required

A defensible investor model must include:
- Historical monthly revenue and cost data
- Services pipeline by probability and expected close date
- Gross margin by engagement type
- Headcount and contractor costs
- Cloud, software and infrastructure costs
- B3 acquisition, support and transaction costs
- Working capital and cash runway
- Base, downside and upside cases
- Funding amount, instrument, milestones and use of funds

No revenue, valuation, growth rate or funding target is assumed in this draft.

## 9. Funding strategy

Capital should be raised against a defined milestone plan, not simply to finance an ambition. Candidate milestones include:
- Establish repeatable paid enterprise engagements
- Demonstrate delivery margins and a predictable sales process
- Validate B3's target segment, retention and exchange liquidity
- Resolve IP ownership, governance and security documentation
- Build a small, accountable delivery team

The appropriate instrument—equity, debt, grant, strategic partnership or bootstrapping—depends on the company's legal structure, cash flows, risk and growth plan.

## 10. Pitch deck structure

1. Cover and one-sentence positioning
2. Customer problem and evidence
3. Target customer segments and market sizing
4. B9TECH solution and service offer
5. B3 product demonstration and validated traction
6. Business model and pricing
7. Go-to-market strategy
8. Competitive landscape and differentiation
9. Delivery capability, team and governance
10. Historical financials and forward model
11. Funding requirement and use of funds
12. Milestones, risks and investment rationale

## 11. Critical risks to disclose

- Unvalidated product-market fit and marketplace liquidity
- Founder and key-person dependency
- Services capacity and sales concentration
- Product security, privacy and data protection
- IP ownership and third-party software dependencies
- Regulatory and contractual obligations
- Cash flow and working-capital constraints

## 12. Investment-readiness gate

Do not circulate an investor deck as an investment proposition until the company has:
1. Verified legal entity and cap table
2. Reconciled historical financials
3. Evidence-backed product and customer metrics
4. IP ownership and material contract review
5. A bottom-up market and competitor assessment
6. A model with transparent assumptions and sensitivities
7. A defined funding ask, instrument and use of funds
