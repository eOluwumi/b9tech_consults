# Nigeria Market & Competitive Landscape — Working Note

**Prepared:** 3 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Inform B9TECH's initial market focus and investor narrative  
**Status:** Desk research only; not a market-sizing report or proof of demand for B9TECH.

## Executive read

Nigeria has a meaningful digital economy and visible enterprise demand for technology, but a large national ICT sector does not automatically translate into an addressable market for a small consultancy. B9TECH should avoid presenting broad ICT GDP as its TAM.

The most credible initial positioning hypothesis is a focused, implementation-led technology partner for Nigerian SMEs and mid-market organisations that need practical data, workflow and AI adoption—not a general-purpose frontier AI laboratory and not a large systems integrator.

B3 should be evaluated as a separate product investment thesis. Its marketplace economics, liquidity and retention need their own evidence.

## Demand-side signals

### 1. ICT is economically material, but broad sector numbers are not B9TECH's addressable market

The Nigerian Investment Promotion Commission's ICT sector overview reports ICT at 18.9% of GDP in 2024 and highlights enterprise software, cybersecurity, AI/data analytics, cloud and e-commerce as opportunity areas. It also notes the concentration of digital and technology activity in Lagos, Abuja and Rivers.

Source: https://nipc.gov.ng/sectors/ict

**Interpretation:** This supports the relevance of technology services in Nigeria. It does not justify using 18.9% of GDP as B9TECH's TAM. A bottom-up market model must define buyer type, sector, use case, average contract value, purchase frequency and realistic serviceable geography.

### 2. SME digital adoption has a practical execution gap

A June 2026 report on Zoho's study of 144 Nigerian SMEs describes infrastructure, broadband and skills constraints as barriers to AI adoption.

Source: https://bizwatchnigeria.ng/ai-adoption-hampered-by-infrastructure-gaps-skills-shortages-among-smes-zoho/

A 2025 SME readiness report from eTranzact and Pan-Atlantic University's Enterprise Development Centre was reported as finding AI adoption remained low, with awareness and readiness varying by sector.

Source: https://businessday.ng/technology/article/nigerian-msmes-keen-on-ai-but-adoption-still-low/

The Development Bank of Nigeria's October 2026 technology research note highlights the distinction between digital access and productive digital depth, including constraints in internet use, fixed broadband and financing for technology MSMEs.

Source: https://data.devbankng.com/stories/technology

**Interpretation:** The opportunity may be less about selling abstract AI and more about solving a measurable operating problem with low-friction, mobile-aware, affordable implementation. These sources are directional; validate the actual buyer problem with interviews and paid pilots.

## Competitive landscape

### Enterprise transformation and systems integration

**Tenece Professional Services** positions itself as a multi-competency enterprise transformation and technology consulting firm, covering IT consulting, business transformation, software engineering, systems integration, process reengineering and managed services.

- Website: https://tenece.com/
- About: https://tenece.com/about-us/
- Services: https://tenece.com/our-services/

**Competitive implication:** Tenece is a stronger established enterprise-services comparator. B9TECH should not claim comparable scale, client reach, delivery capacity or track record without evidence. A plausible differentiation is narrower, founder-led, scoped delivery for organisations not ready to engage a large integrator.

### Frontier AI and local AI infrastructure

**Awarri** publicly presents AI products, local-language AI models, data infrastructure, data labelling, model-training support, RLHF, custom data platforms and partnerships around N-ATLAS.

- Website: https://www.awarri.com/
- AI products: https://www.awarri.com/ai-product
- AI and data services: https://www.awarri.com/ai-services
- Company profile: https://www.awarri.com/company/about-us

The Federal Ministry of Communications, Innovation & Digital Economy announced N-ATLAS in September 2025 and identified Awarri as the partner powering the model.

Source: https://fmcide.gov.ng/nigeria-launches-landmark-ai-model-powered-by-awarri-to-advance-languages-and-ai-at-scale/

**Competitive implication:** Awarri is not merely a generic consulting competitor; it is a differentiated frontier-AI and data-infrastructure player with public institutional partnerships. B9TECH should not position itself as a competitor in local LLM development unless it has a distinct technical asset and evidence. It may instead build useful business applications and integrations on existing models and platforms.

### Boutique AI and software providers

Clutch's Lagos AI company directory lists a mix of specialist AI developers, software agencies and IT providers, including newer small teams. Listings include service mix, employee ranges, review counts and minimum project budgets, but these are directory data and should be independently verified.

Source: https://clutch.co/ng/developers/artificial-intelligence/lagos

**Competitive implication:** The market is fragmented. Buyers can choose from independent consultants, small specialist teams, software agencies and larger integrators. B9TECH needs proof of delivery, a clear service package, response speed, domain focus and trustworthy references to stand out.

## Recommended initial wedge (hypothesis to validate)

Do not market six broad service lines as equally mature. Lead with three tightly connected offers:

1. **Data and workflow diagnostics:** identify process bottlenecks, data quality issues, reporting gaps and automation opportunities.
2. **Practical AI and automation pilots:** scoped proof-of-value projects using appropriate existing models and tools, with human review, security and cost controls.
3. **Business software and analytics implementation:** dashboards, integrations, lightweight internal tools and operational workflows that turn the pilot into a usable system.

Cybersecurity and responsible-AI controls should be embedded in delivery. Do not market penetration testing, formal security certification, regulatory assurance or managed security operations unless qualified capacity and credentials are confirmed.

### Candidate initial customer profile

- Nigerian SMEs and mid-market businesses with an identifiable operational workflow.
- Founder-led or function-led buyers with a clear budget owner.
- Organisations already using spreadsheets, email, WhatsApp or disconnected SaaS tools and experiencing a measurable coordination/reporting problem.
- Initial verticals to test: professional services, distribution/wholesale, retail/e-commerce operations and logistics. These are hypotheses, not selected markets.

### Qualification criteria

Prioritise prospects where:
- The problem occurs frequently and has a measurable cost.
- A decision-maker is accessible.
- The client has usable data and authority to share it.
- The solution can be piloted in 2–6 weeks.
- The client can pay for discovery or pilot delivery.
- The work can become a repeatable package without excessive customisation.

## What must be validated before an investor TAM slide

1. Select two initial verticals and one buyer persona for each.
2. Conduct 15–25 structured customer interviews.
3. Record current workflow, cost of problem, current workaround, decision-maker, budget and urgency.
4. Obtain at least 3–5 paid discovery/pilot engagements before claiming repeatable demand.
5. Measure delivery hours, direct costs, gross margin, time-to-value and conversion to follow-on work.
6. Build TAM/SAM/SOM bottom-up from the number of target organisations, realistic annual spend and achievable share—not from national ICT GDP.

## Investor narrative implications

- Services are the near-term commercial validation engine, if actual contracts and gross margins support it.
- B3 is a separate product thesis; do not combine its potential marketplace value with consulting revenue.
- The strongest story is disciplined execution in a defined market segment, not a broad claim to serve every sector or build every kind of AI.
- A credible moat may emerge from repeatable delivery methods, customer workflow knowledge, integrations, proprietary product IP and distribution. None should be presented as an established moat until demonstrated.

## Source quality and caveats

- NIPC is a government investment-promotion source; use its sector overview as macro context, not a direct serviceable-market calculation.
- DBN research is relevant to technology MSMEs and digital productivity; inspect the full methodology and underlying data before quoting figures in investor materials.
- BusinessDay and BizWatch report findings from external studies; obtain the original reports before relying on specific percentages.
- Company websites describe competitors' own positioning and claims; they are not independent verification of performance.
- Clutch is a commercial directory; use it for competitor discovery, not definitive market share or quality rankings.
